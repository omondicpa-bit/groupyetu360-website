How to Handle Spouses and Family Members in a Chama

Should a couple pay as one member or two? Most groups never decide, and the first dispute decides for them. Here are the setups that work, and the rules to write down.

GroupYetu360 Team · Updated 2026

Sooner or later, a couple wants to join your chama. Or a member asks if their spouse can contribute through them. The answer is rarely written down. So the group decides on the spot, and everyone remembers it differently six months later.

Here are the three setups we see most often, and how to keep each one clear.

Three common setups

1. Both spouses are full members

Each person pays their own contribution, votes on their own, and receives their own payout. This is the simplest to explain. It also costs the household the most, so some groups avoid it.

2. One member pays for the household

One spouse is the member. The other is covered. The household pays through one person, and the records sit under that person's name. This keeps contributions simple, but you must decide who counts as covered.

3. The spouse participates without separate dues

The spouse attends meetings and benefits from welfare, but the principal pays for both. Some groups use this for family members who are not yet ready to join in their own right.

Rules to write down before it comes up

  • Who votes. One vote per household, or one per person?
  • Who receives a payout. In a merry-go-round, does the household get one turn or two?
  • How welfare works. Is cover per person, or per household? A funeral fund is where this matters most.
  • What "behind" means. If the paying member falls behind, is the whole household behind?
  • What happens if the paying member leaves. Does the spouse stay, or does the cover end?
Tip. Write the answers in your constitution or minutes. A decision that is not written down is only a memory, and memories differ.

Where records go wrong

Most household disputes are not about the money. They are about the record. A payment is logged under the wrong spouse. Welfare is counted twice. One person looks behind on paper while the household has paid in full.

The fix is to record household money against one person, and to show clearly who is covered.

How GroupYetu360 handles households

Households is a setting your group can switch on in Features. Switch it on only if you need it.

  • You link a member to the person who pays for them.
  • The money stays recorded against the paying member.
  • In the member list, the covered spouse shows as "Covered by" the paying member.
  • Welfare is tracked per household, for current members only.
  • When the paying member is Active or Behind, participating spouses follow the same status.

A member cannot be linked to themselves. And you cannot link to someone who is already covered by another member. Link to the person who actually pays.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do we have to use Households?

No. It is a setting for each group. Groups where every person pays their own dues can leave it off.

What if the paying member is behind?

Participating household members follow the paying member's status. If the payer is Behind, the household shows as Behind until they catch up.

Can we change a link later?

Yes. An official can change who a member is covered by, or remove the link, from that member's details.
