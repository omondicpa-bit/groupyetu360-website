Product · Michango and Tickets

Harambees and events, run in the open

How Michango and Event Tickets put every gift, payout and ticket on record, and let friends and well-wishers give or buy by M-Pesa without the app.

GroupYetu360 Team · 28 October 2026 · Updated 11 November 2026

Every Kenyan knows how a harambee usually goes. A message goes round the WhatsApp groups with a phone number. Pledges are typed into the chat. M-Pesa messages arrive on the treasurer's phone, and someone sits up late adding them up from screenshots. Weeks later, the questions begin: how much came in, how much reached the hospital, and who still has the balance? Most of the time nobody has done anything wrong. There was simply no clear record.

Michango and Event Tickets were built inside GroupYetu360 to answer those questions before anyone needs to ask them. They are for our users, whether you are an official of a chama, a welfare committee or a church group, or a member organising for a friend. And they reach well beyond the app: the friends, relatives and well-wishers who give or buy a ticket need nothing more than the link and M-Pesa.

Michango: a harambee everyone can see

A Michango is your harambee's own page. You write the story, add a photo, and if you wish set a target and a closing date. Once it is published you have one link to share on WhatsApp, Facebook or by SMS. Three things set it apart from a phone number in a WhatsApp message.

1. Everyone sees the money come in

Each gift appears on the page as it arrives, with the giver's name and message, or as "Anonymous" if they prefer. The progress bar and the total update on their own. Nobody has to collect screenshots, and nobody has to take the treasurer's word for the figure: the page is the record.

2. Everyone sees where the money went

This is where most fundraising falls short, and where Michango is strongest. Under "Where the money went", the page lists every payout with its date, the amount, the M-Pesa receipt and who received it. A giver can see for themselves that Ksh 120,000 went to the hospital's paybill on a given day.

  • The destination is fixed before the page goes live. You choose your own M-Pesa, or pay straight into the hospital's, school's or funeral home's paybill with the patient or student number as the account, or into a business till. Once published, it cannot be changed.
  • Every withdrawal is confirmed with a code sent by SMS to the organiser's phone, so nobody can take the money with a borrowed phone or a guessed password.
  • The organiser's identity is verified before the page goes public, and the page shows "ID verified" to every visitor.
  • Givers can raise a concern. Each page has a "Report this harambee" link. Our team reviews every report, and payouts can be paused while we check.

3. Giving takes less than a minute, with or without the app

A well-wisher in Kisumu, a cousin in Dubai or a colleague who has never heard of GroupYetu360 opens the link, picks an amount, types an M-Pesa number and confirms with their PIN. That is all. Their phone number is never shown on the page, and they receive a thank-you SMS when the gift lands.

Those who would rather not open a link can give from the M-Pesa menu: each Michango has a paybill and its own account number. For a church service, a funeral or a fundraising dinner, the organiser can print an M-Pesa QR code on a poster, and people scan it with the M-Pesa app to give on the spot.

Keeping givers close. The organiser can post updates on the page ("The surgery went well. Thank you all."), so the people who gave hear how it ended, not just how it began.

What it costs

Giving is free for the giver. When money is paid out, 2.5% is kept, and that already covers the M-Pesa charges on the payout. There is no monthly fee and nothing to pay if nothing is raised. Payouts start from Ksh 500.

An example

Akinyi needs surgery costing Ksh 150,000. Her chama's treasurer opens Michango in GroupYetu360, writes her story, sets the target and chooses "Straight to a paybill", entering the hospital's paybill and Akinyi's patient number. The page goes live and the link goes to the chama, the church group and the family. Over ten days, 214 people give, about half of them not GroupYetu360 users. When the total is reached, the treasurer pays it out, confirms with the SMS code, and the hospital receipt appears on the page for every giver to see. There are no screenshots, no spreadsheet and no awkward questions afterwards.

Event tickets: your event, paid and checked in properly

The same thinking applies to events. Whether it is your group's end-year party, a fundraising dinner, a members' trip, a church concert or a tournament, Tickets handles the selling, the money and the door.

For a group's event, the books keep themselves

When a group's admin or treasurer creates an event for the group, every ticket sold sends its money to the group's approved account and records it in the group's books as income, with the fee shown. When the treasurer reports at the next meeting, the figures are already there, ticket by ticket. Members see the event in the app under "Coming up in your groups".

Anyone can buy, without the app

You share one link. Members, friends and the public open it, choose their tickets, enter a name and email, and pay by M-Pesa prompt. Their tickets arrive by email, each with its own QR code, and a link to open them again at any time.

  • Several ticket types. Up to eight per event, such as Regular, VIP, Couple or Student, each with its own price and, if you wish, a limit on how many are sold. Free tickets work too, for events where you only need a headcount.
  • Every sale in one place. The organiser sees each order: who bought, which tickets, and the M-Pesa receipt.
  • Check-in on your phone. At the door, open the event in the app and scan each ticket with the phone camera. Each ticket works once, so a forwarded screenshot cannot get two people in. If the camera fails, type the code printed under the QR.

For individuals and businesses

A person or a registered business can also sell tickets, after verifying their identity (and, for a business, its registration). The money from sales can be withdrawn to M-Pesa, a paybill, a till or a bank paybill once the event begins.

What it costs

3.5% of each paid ticket, with the M-Pesa charges included. Free tickets cost nothing.

Why we built it this way

Community money in Kenya runs on trust, and trust breaks when people cannot see what happened to their contribution. A harambee page that shows every gift and every payout, and an event whose ticket money is booked as it comes in, remove the doubt rather than asking people to set it aside. That is the same promise GroupYetu360 makes to every chama, SACCO and welfare group: clear books that every member can see.

Frequently asked questions

Do givers and ticket buyers need GroupYetu360?

No. They open your link and pay by M-Pesa. Only the organiser uses the app, which is how the page, the payouts and the check-in are managed.

How do I start?

Open GroupYetu360 and tap Michango or Tickets on the home screen. The first time you organise, we ask you to verify your identity.

Can a giver stay anonymous?

Yes. They tick "Give anonymously" and the page shows the gift without their name. Phone numbers are never shown in either case.

Can I change where the harambee money goes after it is live?

No, and that is deliberate. Givers give knowing where the money will go, so the destination is fixed when the page is published.

Can people find my harambee or event without the link?

Only if you choose. You can list a live Michango or event so that people browsing in GroupYetu360 can find it; otherwise only people with the link see it.

Who handles permits and levies for an event?

The organiser. Venue permits, county entertainment levies and taxes remain the organiser's responsibility. See our Terms of Service.
