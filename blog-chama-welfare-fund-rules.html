How a Chama Welfare Fund Works: Rules for Bereavement and Other Events

A welfare fund is the part of a chama people remember most. It is there when someone loses a loved one. These are the rules to agree before that day.

GroupYetu360 Team · Updated 2026

When a member loses a parent, a spouse or a child, the group is expected to show up. With money, and with presence. A welfare fund makes sure the money is ready.

The trouble starts when the rules are not clear. Who is covered? How much is paid? Is a member who is behind still entitled? Decide these before the first event.

What a welfare fund usually covers

  • Bereavement. The most common reason groups start a welfare fund.
  • Illness and hospital bills.
  • Celebrations. Weddings, births and graduations.

Start with bereavement. Add others only if members ask for them.

Two ways to fund it

1. A regular contribution

Every member pays a small amount each month into the welfare fund. When an event happens, the group pays from the pool. This is predictable and fair.

2. A top-up when an event happens

No monthly payment. When someone is bereaved, every member gives a set amount. This feels lighter, but collecting quickly is harder. Records often go missing.

Many groups combine both. A small monthly amount builds a cushion, and a top-up covers larger events.

Rules to write down

  • Who is covered. The member only? Spouse, children, parents?
  • How much is paid. A fixed amount per type of event is easiest to defend.
  • Who qualifies. A new member may need to wait a few months. Say how many.
  • What if a member is behind. Does cover pause until they catch up?
  • Who approves a payout. Name the officials. Record the decision.
  • What happens to leftover money. Does it roll over, or go back to members?
Tip. Settle spouses and dependants early. They are where most welfare disputes begin. Our guide on spouses and family members in a chama explains the options.

Where welfare records go wrong

Welfare money is emotional money. A mistake feels worse than usual. Common problems:

  • Contributions sit in the main account and nobody can say how much is welfare.
  • A payout is agreed at the funeral and never written down.
  • Members are not sure if they have paid for a given event.

Keep welfare money and records separate from your main contributions.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should a welfare contribution be?

Small enough that every member can pay without strain. Look at your likely payouts and divide across members.

Can a member who has left claim welfare?

Only if your rules say so. Most groups cover current members only. Write it down.

Should welfare be a separate fund?

Yes. It keeps the money clear, and members trust it more.
